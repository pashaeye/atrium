Incognito proxy

Incognito windows can go out through a proxy in another country, so sites (and Google’s ads) see you there. Normal windows always stay direct. Needs “Allow in Incognito” for Atrium in chrome://extensions.

Allow Atrium in Incognito first

Chrome doesn’t let the extension into Incognito yet, so the proxy can’t be switched on. Turn on “Allow in Incognito” in the extension’s settings, then come back here.

Free proxies are public servers: slow, often down, and they can see which sites you open (not what’s on HTTPS pages). Fine for checking search results, not for signing in. The country follows the one picked on the Google panel.

Your own proxy

From a paid provider (residential proxies work best with Google) or your own server. SOCKS5 with a login isn’t supported by Chrome; use HTTP for that.
